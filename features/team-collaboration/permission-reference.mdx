What you can do in MemoryLake — what pages you can open, what buttons you can click, what API calls succeed — depends on the permissions your team admin has granted you. This page is your guide:

- Each capability is described in plain language with **what it lets you do** and **what you'll see when it's missing**.
- The exact **permission identifier** is shown in each entry — copy that to your admin if you need a permission added.

### How permissions reach you

Permissions reach an individual member through three layers your admin controls:

1. **Role** — the built-in roles (Owner / Admin / Member) come with a default bundle of capabilities. Owners can also create **custom roles** by cloning a parent role or starting blank. See [Roles and Permissions](/features/team-collaboration/roles-permissions).
2. **Permission policy** — Allow / Deny rules layered on top of the role. Admins use these to override role defaults (e.g. let Members create projects).
3. **Resource scope** — per-member whitelist of specific workspaces, projects, or other resources a member can act on, plus the subset of operations allowed on each. This is how an `instance`-level capability gets narrowed to one or two workspaces or projects.

API Keys are programmatic identities (**Service Accounts**) — each one inherits the role and resource scope of the member who created it. Removing a member revokes every key they own.

## How to spot a missing permission

You don't have to memorize anything. MemoryLake makes missing permissions visible in predictable ways:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="In the console" icon="display">
    - **A tab disappears** from the side menu — that tab needs a permission you don't have.
    - **A button is greyed out** — hover and the tooltip tells you exactly which permission is missing.
    - **A list loads empty** even though you know there should be items in it.
    - **Opening a URL directly** redirects you back, or shows a "no permission" page.
  </Card>
  <Card title="When calling the API" icon="code">
    - Requests are rejected with **HTTP 403** and an `ACCESS_DENIED` error code.
    - The error response tells you which permission is missing.
    - See [Errors](/features/memorylake/api-reference/errors) for the full error envelope.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Tip>If a console button is greyed out and you don't know why, hover over it. The tooltip always names the exact permission you'd need.</Tip>

## How permissions are grouped

Permissions live on seven resources. Every capability below belongs to one of them:

<CardGroup cols={3}>
  <Card title="Workspace" icon="building">
    Your tenant-level data boundary — conversations, workspace-scoped search, and the right to bring actors and agents into it. All `workspace:*` capabilities live here.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Actor in a workspace" icon="user-check">
    One actor's membership of one workspace, and the facts it accumulates there. Granted on the membership itself, not on the workspace. All `workspace_actor:*` capabilities live here.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Agent in a workspace" icon="robot">
    One agent's membership of one workspace — its facts there, and A2A operations run as it. Granted on the membership itself. All `workspace_agent:*` capabilities live here.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Actor" icon="user">
    Individual memory carriers — the people whose facts accumulate across projects. An agent's memories are addressed on the agent, not here. All `actor:*` capabilities live here.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Agent" icon="robot">
    AI agent definitions — model, system prompt, capabilities, and versioned configurations. All `agent:*` capabilities live here.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Project" icon="folder">
    Knowledge domains inside a workspace — documents, project-scoped facts, and MCP servers. All `project:*` capabilities live here.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Library" icon="hard-drive">
    Your shared file space — the storage that sits underneath every project. All `drive:*` capabilities live here.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

### Lists are filtered, not gated

There is no permission for *seeing a list*. Every list endpoint returns the rows you may open and leaves out the rest, so a list is never refused for want of a permission — it comes back shorter. A workspace list shows the workspaces you hold `workspace:read` on, a project list the projects you hold `project:read` on, and so on down each resource.

This replaces an earlier set of `*_list` capabilities, which could be held without the matching `*_read` and produced rows you could see but not open. If you are looking for `project:list`, `workspace:list`, `actor:list`, `agent:list` or any of their siblings, that is why they are gone: holding `*_read` is holding the list.

<Note>
  An empty list and a missing row say the same thing as "you may not open this". Neither is an error, and neither tells you whether the thing exists.
</Note>

## How permissions are granted

Every capability has a **Level** — `instance` or `service` — that determines whether your admin can narrow it down to specific resources. Each entry below shows the level in its **Level** line.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Instance-level" icon="filter">
    Acts on a specific resource instance (one workspace, one project, one actor, one agent). The role's policy makes the action **available**; the member's **resource scope** decides **which instances** they can perform it on — admins can hand out access to workspace A and withhold it on workspace B from the same dialog.

    Covers opening, editing, deleting, and searching within a specific workspace or project, plus all operations on its contents. Marked `instance` in the catalog.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Service-level" icon="globe">
    Acts on the resource type as a whole — there is no specific instance to narrow to. Once a role's policy grants it, it applies team-wide; **resource scope has no effect** on these capabilities.

    - **Browse resource lists** and **create new resources** — these don't act on a specific instance.
    - **All Library capabilities** — Library is one shared file space.

    Marked `service` in the catalog.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Note>If something isn't working in a specific workspace or project, the first question to ask is usually: **do I have permission to open that workspace or project at all?** Instance-level capabilities only do anything inside resources you've been granted access to.</Note>

These are typical capability bundles to use as **starting points** when you're either building a custom role or adding **permission policies** on top of the built-in Member role. They are not pre-built roles — Owners assemble them via **Team > Permission Settings**, then narrow `instance` capabilities to the right resources via the member's **Resource Scope** when assigning the role.

<AccordionGroup>

  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Document curator">

    | `project:mem_modify` | `instance` | Update facts |
    | `project:mem_delete` | `instance` | Forget facts |
    | `project:mem_conflict_resolve` | `instance` | Resolve fact conflicts |
    | `project:mcp_add` | `instance` | Create MCP servers |

  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Workspace administrator">

    | `workspace:read` | `instance` | Open the workspace |
    | `workspace:modify` | `instance` | Edit workspace settings |
    | `workspace:delete` | `instance` | Delete the workspace |
    | `workspace:conv_read` | `instance` | Read conversation history |
    | `workspace:conv_append` | `instance` | Send messages |
    | `workspace:conv_delete` | `instance` | Delete conversations |
    | `workspace_actor:create` | `instance` | Add actors to the workspace |
    | `workspace_actor:delete` | `instance` | Remove actors from the workspace |
    | `workspace_agent:create` | `instance` | Add agents to the workspace |
    | `workspace_agent:delete` | `instance` | Remove agents from the workspace |
    | `workspace_agent:a2a` | `instance` | Run A2A protocol operations |

    Grant the `instance` permissions on **the specific workspaces** the administrator should manage.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Conversation-only integrator">
    The minimum set for an API credential that reads and writes conversations in a workspace without managing the workspace itself.

    | Identifier | Level | Why |
    |---|---|---|
    | `workspace:read` | `instance` | Open the workspace |
    | `workspace:conv_read` | `instance` | Read conversation messages |
    | `workspace:conv_append` | `instance` | Send messages to conversations |

    Add `workspace:conv_delete` if the integration should also clean up old conversations.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Agent operator">
    Can create, configure, and deploy agents across workspaces — but doesn't need project or document access.

    | Identifier | Level | Why |
    |---|---|---|
    | `agent:create` | `service` | Create new agents |
    | `agent:read` | `instance` | View agent configuration |
    | `agent:modify` | `instance` | Update agent identity |
    | `agent:read` | `instance` | See version history |
    | `agent:modify` | `instance` | Publish new versions |
    | `agent:read` | `instance` | Inspect a specific version |
    | `workspace:read` | `instance` | Open the workspace |
    | `workspace_agent:create` | `instance` | Bind agents to workspaces |
    | `workspace_agent:delete` | `instance` | Remove agents from workspaces |

    Add `actor:*` permissions if the operator also manages the actors that agents interact with.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="MCP integrator (API Key for a single project)">
    The minimum capability set for a credential that backs one MCP server and is used by an external client to read project content.

    | Identifier | Level | Why |
    |---|---|---|
    | `workspace:read` | `instance` | Open the workspace the project lives in |
    | `project:read` | `instance` | Open the project |
    | `project:doc_read` | `instance` | Fetch document detail |
    | `project:doc_search` | `instance` | Semantic search over documents |
    | `project:mem_read` | `instance` | Fetch fact detail |
    | `project:mem_search` | `instance` | Search facts |
    | `project:search` | `instance` | Combined document + fact search |

    Add `workspace:conv_append` if the integration should write back conversations that produce facts.

    <Note>An API Key is a **Service Account** — it inherits the role and resource scope of the member who created it. To restrict this key to a single project, set the **creator's** resource scope to that project; you can't scope the key independently. If the creator's role or scope changes, the key follows.</Note>
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Working with workspaces


### Create a new workspace

Lets you start a brand-new workspace from scratch.

**Without it:** "New workspace" buttons are hidden or greyed out.

**Identifier:** `workspace:create`

**Level:** `service` — applies team-wide (not tied to a specific resource instance).

---

### Open a workspace

Lets you open a workspace and view its contents. This permission also gates workspace-scoped search — the [Search Memories](/features/memorylake/api-reference/search/search-memories) API requires it.

**Without it:** workspace cards appear faded and clicking does nothing. Typing the workspace URL directly shows a full-page "no permission" message. Workspace-level search calls return 403.

**Identifier:** `workspace:read`

**Level:** `instance` — granted on a specific workspace.

---

### Edit workspace settings

Lets you rename a workspace, edit its description, and change its configuration.

**Without it:** all "Rename" and "Edit" options are greyed out wherever they appear.

**Identifier:** `workspace:modify`

**Level:** `instance` — granted on a specific workspace.

---

### Delete a workspace

Lets you permanently remove a workspace.

<Warning>Deleting a workspace is destructive — its conversations, actor bindings, agent bindings, and all workspace-scoped data are removed along with it.</Warning>

**Without it:** "Delete" options are greyed out on every workspace card and dialog.

**Identifier:** `workspace:delete`

**Level:** `instance` — granted on a specific workspace.

---

## Managing conversations in a workspace

Conversations are workspace-scoped — they live in a workspace and are linked to one or more projects where extracted facts are stored.


### Read a conversation

Lets you open a conversation and read its messages, including batch-fetching multiple messages at once.

**Without it:** conversation rows appear faded; clicking does nothing.

**Identifier:** `workspace:conv_read`

**Level:** `instance` — granted on a specific workspace.

<Note>This permission alone lets you read existing messages but not add new ones. Sending a message requires the next permission.</Note>

---

### Send a message to a conversation

Lets you append a new message to an existing conversation.

**Without it:** the message input area is disabled and explains it needs the conversation-append permission.

**Identifier:** `workspace:conv_append`

**Level:** `instance` — granted on a specific workspace.

---

### Delete a conversation

Lets you permanently remove a conversation from a workspace.

**Without it:** "Delete" actions on conversations are greyed out.

**Identifier:** `workspace:conv_delete`

**Level:** `instance` — granted on a specific workspace.

---

## Managing actor bindings in a workspace

Actors are created globally, then **bound** to workspaces to operate within them. These permissions control which actors can participate in a workspace.


### Bind an actor to a workspace

Lets you add an existing actor to a workspace so they can participate in conversations and accumulate facts.

**Without it:** the "Add actor" action is greyed out.

**Identifier:** `workspace_actor:create`

**Level:** `instance` — granted on the binding between an actor and a workspace.

---

### Remove an actor from a workspace

Lets you unbind an actor from a workspace — they stop participating, but their globally-stored identity and facts remain.

**Without it:** the per-actor "Remove" action is greyed out.

**Identifier:** `workspace_actor:delete`

**Level:** `instance` — granted on the binding between an actor and a workspace.

---

## Managing actor facts in a workspace

Actor facts are knowledge about a specific person or agent that accumulates across projects within a workspace. These permissions control access to that per-actor knowledge.


### Read an actor fact

Lets you view the full content of a specific actor fact.

**Without it:** fact rows appear faded; clicking does nothing.

**Identifier:** `workspace_actor:fact_read`

**Level:** `instance` — granted on the binding between an actor and a workspace.

---

### Update an actor fact

Lets you edit the content of an actor fact.

**Without it:** "Edit" actions on actor facts are greyed out.

**Identifier:** `workspace_actor:fact_modify`

**Level:** `instance` — granted on the binding between an actor and a workspace.

---

### Forget an actor fact

Lets you permanently remove an actor fact.

**Without it:** "Forget" actions on actor facts are greyed out.

**Identifier:** `workspace_actor:fact_delete`

**Level:** `instance` — granted on the binding between an actor and a workspace.

---

## Managing agent bindings in a workspace

Agents are created globally, then **bound** to workspaces to serve conversations. These permissions control which agents can operate in a workspace.


### Bind an agent to a workspace

Lets you add an existing agent to a workspace so it can serve conversations.

**Without it:** the "Add agent" action is greyed out.

**Identifier:** `workspace_agent:create`

**Level:** `instance` — granted on the binding between an agent and a workspace.

---

### Remove an agent from a workspace

Lets you unbind an agent from a workspace — it stops serving conversations, but its global definition and version history remain.

**Without it:** the per-agent "Remove" action is greyed out.

**Identifier:** `workspace_agent:delete`

**Level:** `instance` — granted on the binding between an agent and a workspace.

---

## A2A protocol operations

### Use the A2A protocol

Lets you initiate and participate in Agent-to-Agent (A2A) protocol operations within a workspace.

**Without it:** A2A requests to this workspace return 403.

**Identifier:** `workspace_agent:a2a`

**Level:** `instance` — granted on the binding between an agent and a workspace.

## Working with agents


### Create an agent

Lets you define a new agent with its model, system prompt, and capabilities. Creating an agent automatically creates a corresponding actor.

**Without it:** "New agent" buttons are hidden or greyed out.

**Identifier:** `agent:create`

**Level:** `service` — applies team-wide (not tied to a specific resource instance).

---

### View an agent

Lets you open an agent and see its configuration — model, system prompt, capabilities, and policies. **Its version history comes with it**: listing past versions and opening any one of them are part of reading the agent, not permissions of their own.

**Without it:** agent rows appear faded; clicking does nothing.

**Identifier:** `agent:read`

**Level:** `instance` — granted on a specific agent.

---

### Edit an agent's identity

Lets you update an agent's identity fields — name, description, and other top-level settings that apply across all versions. **Publishing a new version is the same permission**: a version is a snapshot of the agent, so changing what it will be next is changing the agent.

**Without it:** "Edit" options on the agent's identity are greyed out.

**Identifier:** `agent:modify`

**Level:** `instance` — granted on a specific agent.

<Note>Changing an agent's configuration (model, prompt, capabilities) creates a new **version** — that requires the version-create permission below. This permission only covers identity fields that update in place.</Note>

---

### Delete an agent

Lets you permanently remove an agent and all its versions.

<Warning>Deleting an agent also removes its auto-created actor. This cannot be undone.</Warning>

**Without it:** "Delete" options on agents are greyed out.

**Identifier:** `agent:delete`

**Level:** `instance` — granted on a specific agent.

## Working with documents inside a project


### Read a single document

Lets you fetch full document details through the API.

**Where this matters:** mostly API integrations, plus the console's background poll that refreshes document processing status. Console file downloads go through the **Read your files** permission in the Library section.

**Identifier:** `project:doc_read`

**Level:** `instance` — granted on a specific project.

**Covers this API:** [Get Document](/features/memorylake/api-reference/v3-documents/get-document).

---

### Add a document to a project

Lets you bring a file into a project — through the "Add document" button, the empty-state upload card, and the upload icon inside the chat composer.

**Without it:** every "Add document" / upload entry point is greyed out.

**Identifier:** `project:doc_add`

**Level:** `instance` — granted on a specific project.

<Note>
The [Import Documents](/features/memorylake/api-reference/v3-documents/import-documents) API endpoint actually checks **two** permissions together: `project:doc_add` (this one) **and** [`drive:item_read`](#read-your-files) — the server has to read each referenced Library file before importing it. Without `drive:item_read`, the call returns 403 even though `project:doc_add` is granted.

If you're also **uploading a brand-new file** before importing it, you'll additionally need [`drive:item_add`](#upload-and-create-files) for the Library upload step.
</Note>

---

### Remove a document from a project

Lets you take a document out of a project, one at a time or in bulk.

<Note>Removing a document from a project unlinks it from that project but does **not** delete the underlying file in your Library. Deleting the file itself needs the **Delete files** permission in the Library section below.</Note>

**Without it:** the per-row "Remove" action and the batch-remove action on the Files tab are greyed out.

**Identifier:** `project:doc_delete`

**Level:** `instance` — granted on a specific project.

---

### Search documents inside a project (API-only)

Lets you run semantic search queries over the documents in a project.

**Where this matters:** API integrations only. The console's in-document search is wired to its own controls.

**Identifier:** `project:doc_search`

**Level:** `instance` — granted on a specific project.

## Working with your Library

Your Library is the shared file space that sits underneath every project. When you "add a document to a project", the file goes into your Library first and the project just links to it. The four permissions below apply across all your projects at once — they are not granted per project.

### Read your files

Lets you browse the file tree, see thumbnails, preview files in the viewer, download files, and read the comments people have left on them.

**Without it:** thumbnails fall back to plain file-type icons, "Download" menu items grey out, file previews are skipped, and comment panels degrade silently.

**Identifier:** `drive:item_read`

**Level:** `service` — applies team-wide (not tied to a specific resource instance).

**Covers these APIs:** [Get Item](/features/memorylake/api-reference/library/get-item), [List Items](/features/memorylake/api-reference/library/list-items).

---

### Upload and create files

Lets you upload files, create folders, and attach external storage. This is the permission that runs in the background whenever you click "Add document" or drag a file into the app.

**Without it:** "New folder" and "Upload file" entry points are greyed out everywhere, including inside the "Add document" dialog.

**Identifier:** `drive:item_add`

**Level:** `service` — applies team-wide (not tied to a specific resource instance).

**Covers these APIs:** [Create Upload](/features/memorylake/api-reference/library/create-upload), [Create Item](/features/memorylake/api-reference/library/create-item).

---

### Edit your files

Lets you rename files, move them between folders, edit their metadata and tags, and save comments. This also covers managing custom attributes (xattrs) on files via the API.

**Without it:** rename, move, and edit-metadata options grey out. The "Save" button on the comment panel is disabled and explains it needs the file-edit permission.

**Identifier:** `drive:item_modify`

**Level:** `service` — applies team-wide (not tied to a specific resource instance).

**Covers these APIs:** [Set Item Xattrs](/features/memorylake/api-reference/library/set-item-xattrs), [Delete Item Xattrs](/features/memorylake/api-reference/library/delete-item-xattrs).

<Note>Deleting a tag from a file (removing an xattr) counts as **editing**, not **deleting** — you're changing the file's metadata, not removing the file itself.</Note>

---

### Delete files

Lets you permanently delete files and folders, one or in bulk.

<Warning>A deleted file goes away from every project that linked to it. If you only want to remove the link from one project, use **Remove a document from a project** above instead.</Warning>

**Without it:** all "Delete" actions on files and folders are greyed out.

**Identifier:** `drive:item_delete`

**Level:** `service` — applies team-wide (not tied to a specific resource instance).

**Covers these APIs:** [Delete Item](/features/memorylake/api-reference/library/delete-item).

## Quick troubleshooting

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="I can see the workspace list, but I can't open any of them">
    You have the **Browse workspaces** permission but not the **Open a workspace** one. Ask your admin to grant `workspace:read` on the specific workspace.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The project list is empty, or a project I expect is missing from it">
    The list shows the projects you may open, and nothing else — seeing the list is not a permission of its own. A project you cannot open simply does not appear in it. Ask your admin to grant `project:read` on the project you are looking for, and check that you also have `workspace:read` on the workspace it lives in.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="A project I can open is missing some tabs in the side menu">
    Each tab depends on its own permission:
    - **Files** tab needs `project:doc_read`
    - **Facts** tab needs `project:mem_read`
    - **Integrations** tab needs `project:mcp_read`
    - **Conflicts** tab needs `project:mem_conflict_resolve`
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="A button is greyed out and I don't know why">
    Hover over the button — the tooltip tells you exactly which permission you'd need. Screenshot the tooltip and forward it to your admin.
  </Accordion>



  <Accordion title="I can read a conversation but the message box is disabled">
    You have `workspace:conv_read` but not `workspace:conv_append`. Ask your admin to grant `workspace:conv_append` on this workspace.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="I can't bind an agent to a workspace">
    You need both `agent:read` on the agent **and** `workspace_agent:create` on the workspace. Check that you have both.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="My API call came back with 403 ACCESS_DENIED">
    The response body names the permission, so you do not have to work it out. Read `data.denied[].permission`
    for the identifier, and `data.required_any_of` for what would have satisfied it — granting any one of those
    entries is enough. `data.stage` tells you which ask to make: `capability` means the role itself is missing
    the permission, `instance` means the role has it but this particular workspace or project is outside your
    resource scope. See [Errors](/features/memorylake/api-reference/errors) for the full body.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="I asked my admin for a permission — how do they actually grant it?">
    Admins reach into one of three places depending on what's needed:

    - **Permission policy** (under **Team > Permission Settings**) — to enable a capability your role doesn't include by default. The admin adds an Allow rule with the identifier you sent.
    - **Resource scope** (under **Members > [you] > Set resource scope...**) — to extend an `instance`-level capability to a specific workspace, project, actor, or agent, or to narrow it down.
    - **Role change** (under **Members > [you] > Assign role...**) — if the capability is already in a bigger role and they'd rather hand you that whole role.

    Owners can do all three. Admins can do the first two but **can't promote you** to Admin or to a custom role — that's Owner-only.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Typing a URL I can't access — what happens?">
    - The **workspace or project home page** shows a full-page "no permission" message with a contact-admin link.
    - **A tab inside a resource you can't see** redirects you back to the default tab.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

If the capability you need doesn't appear on your role, contact your team's owner or administrator. They can adjust roles in **Teams > My Teams > Members**.
